RCA Console — how a forecast miss is explained

Business & executive view · every figure verified against the running system, 2026-08-24

Every week about 400 queues get a contact-forecast, and 63% of them miss by more than the agreed tolerance. Working out why any one of them missed is currently manual: an analyst pulls history, checks the calendar, looks at shipments, forms a view. It takes hours, and two analysts reach different answers. This system does that reasoning in about a minute, identically every time — and it is built so that it cannot bluff: the arithmetic is in code, the AI is allowed to choose words but never a number, and the system states its own limits on screen.

Where the data comes from — one read-only SQL Server source

No spreadsheets, no copies, no manual steps. The system cannot change it. Weekly forecast and actuals per queue, plus installed base and shipments, and a public-holiday calendar with curated mapping so one holiday spelled several ways counts once.

The six questions it asks of the data

  • What has this queue done for three years?
  • What happened in the weeks straight after?
  • This queue, or the whole region? (six levels)
  • Did demand move to another channel?
  • Which other queues belong with this one?
  • Was there a public holiday nobody counted?

Why the answer can be trusted

  • Deterministic — same input, same answer, every time.
  • The AI cannot add a number. One unsupported figure discards the whole summary — not flagged, discarded.
  • The analysis never depends on the AI. If every call fails, all figures, causes, confidence and recommendations remain.
  • “Not measurable” is never reported as “no effect” — it says what it needs instead.
  • Small misses are set aside on purpose — a 90% miss on 20 contacts is arithmetic.
  • Every finding names the field it came from.
Scale

427 queues · 49 countries · 5 channels · over 5 years of weekly history

One real investigation

A US social-media queue, 2024. Planned 18,932, arrived 25,697 — short by 6,765 (35.7%). Impact Critical, cause a drifting baseline, confidence 67.5%, 23 explanations tested.

“The evidence implied the plan should have moved by 1,241 contacts. It moved by 77.”

What it cannot do

No daily detail — the source records whole weeks; daily analysis needs a new feed. Promotions and campaigns cannot be tested as causes — that source is not deployed. 185 holiday-naming questions await a business decision. It does not prove causation — it reports what the evidence supports. The judgement stays with the analyst.

Built on a Python service and one self-contained web page. Four dependencies in total — no data-science stack, no AI vendor SDK, so every statistic is plain readable code and no provider is locked in.
